TRANSPARENCY / METHODOLOGY

Read the fine print.

What this platform shows, how the machine-learning layer works and where its limitations lie.

01

Official source

Recorded street-level crime data comes from the public UK Police API. Incidents are released by month, sometimes with a significant delay. They are not real-time alerts.

Police API documentation ↗
02

Spatial uncertainty

Police coordinates are deliberately anonymised and approximate. Hotspots represent patterns in mapped records, not precise crime scenes, offender locations or the actual boundaries of safe places.

About the data ↗
03

Machine learning

An unsupervised Gaussian KDE is fitted to incident coordinates. A percentile compares the result with a grid around the search point. It does not predict individual incidents or personal safety.

Kernel density estimation ↗
04

Privacy & alerts

Geolocation requires explicit permission and is processed in the active browser tab. A new police-data query uses your coordinates when you change the selected area. No account or background tracking is provided.

Official police website ↗
UK coverage note

Street-level coverage is broadly intended for England, Wales and Northern Ireland. In Scotland only limited British Transport Police data may appear. A low count does not mean an area is safe. Population, reporting rates, time of day and local context are not controlled for.
